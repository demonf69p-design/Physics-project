01 / Project overview

What is a jet engine?

A jet engine is a machine that produces thrust by accelerating a stream of gas backwards. It works on Newton’s Third Law of Motion: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.

01 — PRINCIPLE

Newton’s Third Law

The engine pushes hot gases backwards. In reaction, the engine and aircraft are pushed forwards.

02 — ENERGY

Energy conversion

Chemical energy in fuel becomes thermal energy, then kinetic energy in fast-moving exhaust gases.

03 — PURPOSE

Powerful thrust

The thrust generated by the engine overcomes drag and helps an aircraft accelerate and fly.

02 / The science

How does it work?

A typical turbojet follows four main stages. Air enters the front, gets compressed, mixes with fuel and burns, and finally leaves at high speed through the nozzle.

The key idea: The engine does not push against the air outside like a propeller. It creates thrust by changing the momentum of the air flowing through it.

04 / Beyond the classroom

Where are jet engines used?

Jet propulsion has transformed modern aviation and space technology.

AVIATION

Passenger aircraft

Turbofan engines power many commercial airliners, moving large amounts of air efficiently.

DEFENCE

Military aircraft

Turbojet and turbofan designs are used in many high-performance aircraft.

SPACE

Rocket comparison

Jet engines need atmospheric oxygen. Rockets carry their own oxidizer, allowing operation in space.

06 / Conclusion

From physics to flight.

A jet engine is an impressive application of physics. By compressing air, adding energy through combustion and accelerating exhaust gases, it produces thrust. It brings together Newton’s laws, energy conversion, fluid motion and thermodynamics in one remarkable machine.
